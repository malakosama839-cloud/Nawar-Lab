The Compton Effect & E = mc²

A photon bounces off an electron like a billiard ball — the strongest proof that light carries momentum.

The Compton Effect — a Photon Collision

In 1923 Arthur Compton directed X-rays at a block of material containing free (loosely bound) electrons. He studied the scattered light and found something strange: along with the original wavelength, the scattered beam contained light of a longer wavelength (lower frequency, lower energy).

The explanation? Treat the photon as a particle that collides with the electron exactly like two billiard balls, conserving momentum and energy:

  • The photon hits the electron and gives part of its energy to it.
  • Both are scattered — the photon at one angle, the electron at another.
  • Since the photon lost energy, its frequency drops and its wavelength increases: λ′ > λ.

This is the Compton effect — direct, laboratory proof of the particle nature of light. (Waves cannot "bounce" off an electron and come out longer!)

Note: the increase in wavelength depends only on the scattering angle, not on the incident wavelength. The maximum possible increase is Δλ = h/(me·c) = 6.625×10⁻³⁴ / (9.1×10⁻³¹ × 3×10⁸) ≈ 2.4 × 10⁻¹² m — tiny, which is why the effect is only measurable with short-wavelength X-rays.

Photon Momentum

To collide, a photon must carry momentum. From Einstein's energy–momentum relation, a photon of energy E has momentum:

p = E / c = h·f / c = h / λE in joules ⇒ p in kg·m/s. Shorter wavelength ⇒ bigger momentum
Worked example: An X-ray photon has energy E = 1240 eV (that is λ = 1 nm). Find its momentum.
E = 1240 × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 1.984×10⁻¹⁶ J
p = E / c = 1.984×10⁻¹⁶ / 3×10⁸ ≈ 6.6 × 10⁻²⁵ kg·m/s

Fire a photon at an electron at rest — watch both scatter

Momentum and energy are both conserved — exactly like a particle collision.

Mass–Energy Equivalence: E = mc²

Einstein's relativity adds the final piece: mass is a form of energy. A mass m at rest holds an energy

E = m × c²c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s — even 1 gram of matter stores 9 × 10¹³ J of energy

This is why a photon (which has energy) can be said to have a "mass equivalent" m = E/c², and why pairs of particles can be created from energy (and annihilated back into it). The photon has zero rest mass, but its energy and momentum are perfectly real — as Compton's experiment proved.

Worked example: Find the rest energy of an electron (m = 9.1×10⁻³¹ kg) in joules and in eV.
E = m·c² = 9.1×10⁻³¹ × (3×10⁸)² = 9.1×10⁻³¹ × 9×10¹⁶ = 8.19 × 10⁻¹⁴ J
E = 8.19×10⁻¹⁴ / 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ ≈ 5.1×10⁵ eV ≈ 0.51 MeV
