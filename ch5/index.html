Chapter 5: Wave-Particle Duality

Light behaves as a wave and as particles — and matter does too. This is the doorway to quantum physics.

Planck's constant h = 6.625×10⁻³⁴ J·s
hc ≈ 1240 eV·nm
Speed of light c = 3×10⁸ m/s
Electron mass 9.1×10⁻³¹ kg

In this chapter, classical physics breaks down. A hot body refuses to radiate infinite ultraviolet energy (Planck's quanta), light knocks electrons out of metal in a way waves never could (Einstein's photons), photons bounce off electrons like billiard balls (Compton), and finally electrons diffract like waves (de Broglie). One idea ties it all together: energy comes in packets, and everything has both wave and particle faces.
