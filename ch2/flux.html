Magnetic Flux & Flux Density

Magnetic Field Lines

A magnetic field is the region around a magnet (or a current) where a magnetic force can be detected. We picture it using magnetic field lines:

  • Outside a magnet, field lines run from the north pole to the south pole.
  • The direction of the field at any point is the direction a free north pole would move — the tangent to the line.
  • Field lines never cross, and they are closer together where the field is stronger.
  • Two neighbouring field lines never intersect; they also repel each other sideways — that is why lines between like poles spread apart.

Flux Density (B)

The magnetic flux density B measures how strong the field is: it is the number of field lines crossing each square metre perpendicular to the field. Its unit is the tesla (T), where 1 T = 1 Wb/m². A strong lab magnet is about 1 T; the Earth's field is only about 4 × 10⁻⁵ T.

If the field is uniform (equal strength everywhere, parallel lines), the field lines crossing a surface form a straight "bundle" — counting them gives us the next idea: magnetic flux.

Magnetic Flux (Φ)

Magnetic flux is the total number of field lines passing through a surface. Its unit is the weber (Wb). If a uniform field B meets a flat surface of area A, and the angle between B and the normal (the perpendicular line) to the surface is θ, then:

Φ = B × A × cos θΦ in weber (Wb), B in tesla (T), A in m², θ = angle between B and the NORMAL to the surface
Exam trap: θ is measured between B and the normal to the surface — not between B and the surface itself. If the question gives the angle with the plane of the surface, use cos(90° − that angle) = sin(angle) instead.

Maximum and minimum flux

  • Maximum flux (Φ = B·A) when θ = 0°, i.e. B is perpendicular to the surface (parallel to its normal) — the surface faces the field head-on.
  • Zero flux (Φ = 0) when θ = 90°, i.e. B is parallel to the surface (the normal is ⊥ to B) — the lines slide past without crossing it.
Worked example: A uniform field of 0.5 T passes through a rectangular surface of area 0.2 m². The field makes an angle of 60° with the normal to the surface. Find the magnetic flux.
Φ = B·A·cos θ = 0.5 × 0.2 × cos 60° = 0.1 × 0.5 = 0.05 Wb = 50 mWb
(If instead the field were perpendicular to the surface, Φ = 0.5 × 0.2 × cos 0° = 0.1 Wb — the maximum.)
