Chapter 2: Magnetic Effect of Electric Current

Every electric current is surrounded by a magnetic field — Oersted's discovery that connected electricity and magnetism for the first time. In this chapter you will learn how to calculate that field around wires, coils and solenoids, how the field pushes on currents and spins coils (the motor principle), and how we exploit all of this to measure current and voltage with the galvanometer, ammeter, voltmeter and ohmmeter.

By the end of this chapter you can…

  • Define magnetic flux density B (tesla) and magnetic flux Φ (weber), and use Φ = B A cosθ, identifying the conditions for maximum and minimum flux.
  • Use the right-hand grip rule and calculate the field of a straight wire (B = μ₀I/2πr), locate neutral points (Bwire = BEarth), the centre of a circular coil (B = μ₀NI/2r) and the uniform field inside a solenoid (B = μ₀nI).
  • Calculate the force on a straight wire (F = B I L sinθ), determine its direction with Fleming's left-hand rule, and find the force per metre between parallel wires (F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/2πd) with its attraction/repulsion rule.
  • Calculate the torque on a rectangular coil (τ = B I A N sinθ) using the angle with the coil's normal, state when it is maximum, and compute the magnetic dipole moment m = N I A.
  • Describe the moving-coil galvanometer and its radial field, then convert it to a DC ammeter (shunt S = I_g G/(I − I_g)) or a DC voltmeter (R_m = V/I_g − G), and explain the ohmmeter's zero-adjustment, backwards scale and mid-scale reading.
