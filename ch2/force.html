Magnetic Force

Force on a Straight Current-Carrying Wire

Place a wire carrying current I of length L inside a magnetic field B, with the angle between the wire and B equal to θ: the field pushes the wire sideways with a force

F = B × I × L × sin θF in newtons (N). θ is the angle between the WIRE and B. Force is ⊥ to both the wire and B
  • Maximum force F = B·I·L when the wire is perpendicular to B (θ = 90°).
  • Zero force when the wire is parallel to B (θ = 0°) — the field cannot push along itself.
Exam trap: in F = B I L sinθ the angle is between the wire and B. In Φ = B A cosθ the angle is between B and the normal. Check which one the question means!

Fleming's Left-Hand Rule

The direction of the force is found by holding your left hand flat with the thumb, first finger and second finger all at right angles to each other:

  • First finger (index) → points along the Field (N → S).
  • Second finger (middle) → points along the Current (conventional + → −).
  • Thumb → points along the force / Motion of the wire.

Memory aid: Field – Current – Motion for the First, seCond and thuMb. This rule explains why an electric motor spins: current + field → sideways push.

Worked example: A horizontal wire of length 0.5 m carries a current of 3 A perpendicular to a field of 0.4 T. Find the force on it, and the force if the wire is tilted so θ = 30°.
θ = 90°: F = B I L sin90° = 0.4 × 3 × 0.5 × 1 = 0.6 N
θ = 30°: F = 0.4 × 3 × 0.5 × sin30° = 0.6 × 0.5 = 0.3 N

Force Between Two Parallel Wires

Wire 1's field acts on the current of wire 2, so two parallel wires a distance d apart exert equal and opposite forces on each other. For wires of length L carrying currents I₁ and I₂:

F / L = μ₀ × I₁ × I₂ / (2π d)F per metre of wire (N/m). Same direction currents → attraction. Opposite directions → repulsion
Exam trap: parallel currents in the same direction attract — opposite to what you might guess from charges! This idea is also used to define the ampere.
Worked example: Two parallel wires 0.1 m apart each carry 5 A in the same direction. Find the force per metre between them.
F/L = μ₀ I₁ I₂ / (2π d) = (4π×10⁻⁷ × 5 × 5) / (2π × 0.1) = (2×10⁻⁷ × 25) / 0.1 = 5×10⁻⁶ / 0.1 = 5 × 10⁻⁵ N/m (attraction)
