Properties & Applications of Laser Light

Four properties that ordinary light simply cannot match — and the jobs that follow from them.

The Four Properties (vs Ordinary Light)

PropertyLaser lightOrdinary light
Monochromaticity One single wavelength — one perfectly sharp colour (stimulated emission copies the same transition exactly). A mixture of many wavelengths (white light is a rainbow of colours).
Coherence All photons have the same phase and stay in step — wave crests line up over long distances. This is the key difference. Random phases, constantly changing — atoms emit independently (spontaneous emission).
Collimation (parallelism) The beam stays almost perfectly parallel — it travels kilometres while spreading by only centimetres. Light from a lamp spreads out in all directions; a torch cone widens within metres.
High intensity Energy concentrated in a very narrow beam of one colour — enormous power per cm², enough to cut metal. Energy spread over all directions and all wavelengths — low intensity everywhere.

All four come from the same root: stimulated emission produces identical photons, and the cavity keeps selecting them.

Worked example: Compare the intensities. A small He-Ne laser emits P = 2 mW focused in a spot of area A = 1 mm² = 1×10⁻⁶ m².
Ilaser = P/A = 2×10⁻³ / 1×10⁻⁶ = 2000 W/m².
A 100 W lamp spreads its light over a sphere of radius 2 m: A = 4πr² = 4 × 3.1416 × 4 ≈ 50.3 m².
Ilamp = 100 / 50.3 ≈ 2 W/m² — a thousand times weaker, even though the lamp uses 50 000 times more power!

Applications

  • Holography: 3D imaging that records the phase of the light as well as its intensity, using laser interference — impossible with ordinary (incoherent) light. Holograms are stored on the same principle as credit-card security stickers.
  • Medicine: eye surgery (re-attaching the retina, shaping the cornea to correct vision), bloodless cutting and sealing of tissue, destroying tumours.
  • Industry: cutting, drilling and welding metals, precise length measurement and alignment, reading barcodes in shops, reading CDs/DVDs.
  • Communications: laser light carries data through thin optical fibres — its enormous frequency means an enormous data capacity, and the fibres guide it around corners with almost no loss.
Exam tip: If asked "what distinguishes laser light from ordinary light?", lead with coherence — it is the direct consequence of stimulated emission — then add monochromaticity, collimation and high intensity. For holography specifically, the answer is that the laser's coherence lets the hologram record the phase of light, not just intensity.
