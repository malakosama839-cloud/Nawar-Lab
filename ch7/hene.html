Part II · Chapter 7

The Helium-Neon Laser

Helium does the pumping, neon does the lasing — and out comes the famous red beam.

The Setup

A glass tube contains a gas mixture of helium and neon in about a 10 : 1 ratio at low pressure, with an electrode at each end. An electric discharge (a high voltage current) runs through the gas — this is the electrical pumping of the laser. The tube is closed between two parallel mirrors: one fully reflecting, one partially transparent — the resonant cavity.

Step by Step: How the Red Photon Is Born

Step 1 — Helium is excited

Electrons in the discharge collide with helium atoms and lift them to a long-lived level at 20.61 eV. Helium is ideal because this level is metastable — the atoms stay there.

Step 2 — Collisions transfer the energy

Neon happens to have an excited level at almost exactly the same energy: 20.66 eV (only 0.05 eV higher). When an excited helium atom collides with a relaxed neon atom, it hands over its 20.61 eV (the tiny difference comes from the collision's kinetic energy) and falls back to its ground state. Neon takes its place at 20.66 eV.

Step 3 — Population inversion in neon

Because helium keeps feeding it, neon accumulates more atoms at 20.66 eV than at the lower levels — the population inversion needed for amplification is built in the neon. This is why we say helium acts as the pumping agent for neon.

Step 4 — The red transition

A neon atom drops from 20.66 eV down to a level 1.96 eV below (at 18.70 eV), emitting a photon. Stimulated emission in the cavity amplifies exactly this transition, and the beam escapes: the red He-Ne laser light.

λ = hc / ΔE = 1240 / ΔEΔE in eV gives λ in nm — here ΔE = 1.96 eV
λ = 1240 / 1.96 ≈ 632.8 nmthe standard red He-Ne laser line
Worked example: The neon transition drops ΔE = 1.96 eV. Find the wavelength and frequency of the emitted light.
In electron-volts: λ = hc/ΔE = 1240 / 1.96 ≈ 633 nm — the standard 632.8 nm red line.
In joules: ΔE = 1.96 × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 3.14×10⁻¹⁹ J, so
λ = hc/ΔE = (6.625×10⁻³⁴ × 3×10⁸) / (3.14×10⁻¹⁹) ≈ 6.33×10⁻⁷ m ≈ 633 nm ✓
Frequency: f = c/λ = 3×10⁸ / 6.33×10⁻⁷ ≈ 4.7×10¹⁴ Hz.

Exam tip: Don't mix up the numbers: helium is excited to 20.61 eV, neon's matching level is at 20.66 eV, and the laser transition in neon is 1.96 eV (20.66 → 18.70). And remember the mixture ratio ≈ 10 : 1 (He : Ne).
