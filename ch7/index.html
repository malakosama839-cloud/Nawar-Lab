Chapter 7: Lasers

Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation — making photons march in perfect step.

Planck's constant h = 6.625×10⁻³⁴ J·s
Speed of light c = 3×10⁸ m/s
hc ≈ 1240 eV·nm
He-Ne red line λ ≈ 632.8 nm

Ordinary light sources are chaotic: every atom emits a photon whenever it feels like it, in a random direction with a random phase. A laser forces the opposite. One photon stimulates an excited atom to release an identical twin, twins make more twins inside a mirrored cavity, and out comes a beam that is one single colour, perfectly in step, almost perfectly parallel and astonishingly intense. This chapter builds the laser piece by piece: the three emission processes, the elements every laser needs, the classic helium–neon laser, and the properties that give laser light its jobs.

The He-Ne Laser

Helium pumps neon by collisions (20.61 eV → 20.66 eV), neon falls 1.96 eV and emits red light: λ = 1240/1.96 ≈ 632.8 nm.

Properties & Uses

Monochromaticity, coherence, collimation and high intensity — contrasted with ordinary light — and where lasers earn their keep.

By the end of this chapter you can…

  • Distinguish absorption, spontaneous emission and stimulated emission, and explain why stimulated emission amplifies light.
  • Name the elements of any laser: active medium, pumping source (optical or electrical), metastable state, population inversion and resonant cavity.
  • Explain population inversion (more atoms excited than relaxed) and why the metastable state makes it possible.
  • Describe the He-Ne laser: the 10:1 mixture, helium's 20.61 eV level, the collision transfer to neon's 20.66 eV level, and the 1.96 eV red transition.
  • Calculate photon wavelengths from energy gaps using λ = hc/E = 1240/ΔE, and list the four properties and main applications of laser light.
