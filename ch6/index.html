Chapter 6: Atomic Spectra

Atoms emit light with fingerprints — the key that unlocked the structure of the atom.

Hydrogen levels E_n = −13.6/n² eV
Ionization 13.6 eV
λ from ΔE: λ = 1240/ΔE nm
X-ray cutoff λ_min = hc/eV

Why do atoms glow in sharp colours instead of a smooth rainbow? Bohr answered with energy levels and jumps: each spectral line is one electron jump between two allowed orbits. In this chapter you map hydrogen's five spectral series, learn the three types of spectra and the spectrometer that separates them, and finish with X-rays — where fast electrons hitting a target produce both a continuous spectrum and a target's characteristic lines.

By the end of this chapter you can…

  • State Bohr's postulates and use mvr = n·h/2π and hf = E₂ − E₁.
  • Calculate hydrogen energy levels from E_n = −13.6/n² eV and the ionization energy.
  • Convert a jump's ΔE into a wavelength (λ = 1240/ΔE) and place it in the correct spectral series and region.
  • Distinguish continuous, line-emission and absorption spectra, and explain how a spectrometer produces pure spectra.
  • Describe X-ray production in the Coolidge tube and compute λ_min = hc/eV, contrasting it with the characteristic lines.
