Ohm's Law for a Closed Circuit

The Real Battery

A real battery is not just a voltage source — inside it, the moving charge collides with the battery's own internal parts. So a real battery has an EMF and a small internal resistance (r).

  • EMF (E or VB): the total energy the battery gives to each coulomb. This is what a voltmeter reads when no current flows (key open).
  • Terminal voltage (V): what the voltmeter reads when current actually flows. It is less than the EMF because some energy is "lost" inside the battery.
  • Lost volts (Vlost): the voltage dropped across the internal resistance.
I = EMF / (R + r)the whole loop: external load R plus internal r
V = EMF − I × r   =   I × Rterminal voltage = EMF minus the lost volts
Key idea: as you draw more current (smaller R), the lost volts I·r grow, so the terminal voltage falls. With the circuit open (I = 0), V = EMF exactly.
Worked example: A battery has EMF 12 V and internal resistance 0.5 Ω. It is connected to a load of 5.5 Ω.
I = 12 / (5.5 + 0.5) = 2 A
V = 12 − 2 × 0.5 = 11 V (lost volts = 1 V)
