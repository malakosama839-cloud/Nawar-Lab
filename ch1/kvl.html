Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL)

The Loop Rule

Kirchhoff's Voltage Law deals with any closed loop in a circuit. The law says:

Σ V = 0  around any closed loopVoltage rises (batteries) = voltage drops (resistors), always

This is the conservation of energy: a coulomb of charge gains energy passing through the battery, and it must give up exactly that much energy passing through the resistors — otherwise it would return to the battery with extra energy, which is impossible.

For a single loop with one battery:

EMF = I·R₁ + I·R₂ + I·r + …Every volt gained in the battery is spent across the resistances
Sign convention: going around the loop, take a battery from − to + as +EMF, and cross a resistor in the direction of the current as −I·R (a drop). The sum must cancel to zero.
Worked example: A 12 V battery drives a loop with R₁ = 4 Ω and R₂ = 8 Ω. Verify KVL.
I = 12 / (4 + 8) = 1 A.
Drop on R₁ = 1 × 4 = 4 V; drop on R₂ = 1 × 8 = 8 V.
KVL: +12 − 4 − 8 = 0 ✓
