Electricity — Basic Concepts

The Four Key Quantities

1. Electric Charge (Q)

Charge is the fundamental property of matter that makes electricity happen. There are two kinds: positive (protons) and negative (electrons). Like charges repel, opposite charges attract. Charge is measured in coulombs (C).

2. Electric Current (I)

Current is the rate of flow of electric charge — how many coulombs pass a point in the wire each second. It is measured in amperes (A). Although electrons actually drift from − to +, we traditionally draw conventional current flowing from + to −.

3. Potential Difference / Voltage (V)

Voltage is the energy given to each coulomb of charge — the "push" that drives the current. A battery of 6 V gives 6 joules of energy to every coulomb that passes through it. Measured in volts (V).

4. Resistance (R)

Resistance is the opposition to the flow of current. A thin wire resists more than a thick one; iron resists more than copper. Measured in ohms (Ω).

Ohm's Law

For a metallic conductor at constant temperature, the current is directly proportional to the voltage across it:

V = I × RV in volts, I in amperes, R in ohms
  • Bigger voltage → bigger current (if R is fixed).
  • Bigger resistance → smaller current (if V is fixed).
Worked example: A resistor of 10 Ω is connected to a 6 V battery. Find the current.
I = V / R = 6 / 10 = 0.6 A
