Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL)

The Junction Rule

Kirchhoff's Current Law deals with a junction (node) — a point where three or more wires meet. The law says:

Σ Iin = Σ IoutThe total current entering a junction equals the total current leaving it

This is really the conservation of charge: charge cannot pile up at a point or vanish from it. Whatever flows in per second must flow out per second — just like water in pipe junctions.

Sign convention: in algebraic form (Σ I = 0), currents entering are taken as positive and currents leaving as negative (or the reverse — just be consistent!).
Worked example: At a junction, 3 A and 2 A flow in, while 1.5 A flows out through one wire. Find the current in the last wire.
In: 3 + 2 = 5 A. Out: 1.5 + I → I = 5 − 1.5 = 3.5 A leaving.
