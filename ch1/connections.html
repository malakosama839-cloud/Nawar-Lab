Series & Parallel Connection — Equivalent Resistance

Series Connection

Resistors are in series when they are connected end-to-end so the same current must pass through every one of them, one after the other.

Req = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + …Equivalent resistance is always BIGGER than the largest resistor
  • Same current I through all resistors.
  • Total voltage is shared: V = V₁ + V₂ + V₃ …
  • The biggest resistor takes the biggest share of the voltage.

Parallel Connection

Resistors are in parallel when they are connected across the same two points, so the voltage across each is the same while the current splits between them.

1 / Req = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ + …For two resistors, shortcut: Req = R₁R₂ / (R₁ + R₂)
  • Same voltage V across all resistors.
  • Total current splits: I = I₁ + I₂ + I₃ …
  • Equivalent resistance is always SMALLER than the smallest resistor.
Exam trap: students often mix the two rules. Remember: series → just add. Parallel → add the reciprocals, then flip.
Worked example: R₁ = 6 Ω and R₂ = 3 Ω.
In series: Req = 6 + 3 = 9 Ω.
In parallel: Req = (6 × 3)/(6 + 3) = 18/9 = 2 Ω — smaller than both!
