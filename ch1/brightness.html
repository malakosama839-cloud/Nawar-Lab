Brightness & Keys Ideas

What Makes a Bulb Glow Brighter?

The brightness of a bulb depends on the electrical power it consumes — a bulb is just a resistor (the filament) that glows when it gets hot. Power depends on the current through it:

P = I² × RbulbMore current → more power → brighter glow
  • Two identical bulbs in series: each gets half the voltage → much dimmer.
  • Two identical bulbs in parallel: each gets the full voltage → both shine at full brightness.
  • A battery with high internal resistance gives dimmer bulbs, because some voltage is lost inside the battery.

The Key (Switch)

A key is simply a switch that can open or close the path of the current:

  • Closed key: the circuit is complete → current flows → the bulb lights.
  • Open key: the path is broken → I = 0 → the bulb is off, and the voltmeter across the battery reads the full EMF (no lost volts!).
  • A key in series with a bulb controls that bulb. A key in parallel with a bulb "short-circuits" it — current takes the easy path and the bulb goes out.
Exam favourite: when the key opens, I = 0 everywhere in that loop, so lost volts = 0 and the terminal voltage jumps up to the EMF.
Worked example: Two identical bulbs, each 6 Ω, are connected in series to a 12 V battery.
Req = 12 Ω → I = 1 A → each bulb gets P = I²R = 1² × 6 = 6 W (half brightness).
Now connect them in parallel: each gets 12 V directly → P = V²/R = 144/6 = 24 W each — full brightness!
