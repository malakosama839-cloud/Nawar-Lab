AC with R, L and C

The same alternating current behaves completely differently through a resistor, a coil and a capacitor.

Phasor Diagrams — a 30-Second Guide

In AC circuits, voltages and currents are sine waves of the same frequency. To compare them we draw each as a phasor: an arrow that rotates counter-clockwise. The length of the arrow is the peak value, and the angle between two arrows is the phase difference φ between the two waves. If two phasors point the same way, the waves rise and fall together ("in phase", φ = 0). If one points 90° ahead of the other, it leads by 90° (its peak happens a quarter cycle earlier).

1. Pure Ohmic Resistance (R)

A resistor obeys V = I·R at every instant. When the voltage peaks, the current peaks — at the same moment. Voltage and current are in phase:

φ = 0  ,  VR = I·Rthe phasors V and I point in the same direction — the resistor is the "well-behaved" component

2. Pure Inductive Coil (L)

A coil produces a back-EMF that opposes any change of current (self-induction). So the current cannot keep up with the voltage: it lags behind. The voltage leads the current by 90°.

The coil opposes AC with the inductive reactance:

XL = 2π f Lohms — grows with frequency: a coil blocks high frequencies

At DC (f = 0) the reactance is zero — a pure coil passes DC as if it were just a plain wire. The faster the current alternates, the harder the coil fights it.

3. Pure Capacitor (C)

A capacitor must charge and discharge, and current has to flow onto its plates before the voltage across it can build up. So here the current leads the voltage by 90°.

The capacitor opposes AC with the capacitive reactance:

XC = 1 / (2π f C)ohms — shrinks with frequency: a capacitor passes high frequencies

At DC (f = 0) the reactance is infinite — once charged, the capacitor blocks any steady current. The faster the voltage changes, the more easily charge flows on and off the plates.

Worked example: A pure coil of L = 0.5 H and a pure capacitor of C = 50 μF are each connected alone to a 220 V (RMS), 50 Hz supply.
Coil: XL = 2πfL = 2 × 3.1416 × 50 × 0.5 ≈ 157 Ω → I = 220 / 157 ≈ 1.40 A (current lags V by 90°).
Capacitor: XC = 1/(2πfC) = 1 / (2 × 3.1416 × 50 × 50×10⁻⁶) ≈ 63.7 Ω → I = 220 / 63.7 ≈ 3.46 A (current leads V by 90°).
