Faraday's Law & Lenz's Rule

First, Remember Magnetic Flux

Induction is all about magnetic flux (Φ) — the number of magnetic field lines passing through the area of a coil:

Φ = B × A × cos θΦ in weber (Wb), B in tesla (T), A in m², θ = angle between B and the normal to the coil's face

Flux is maximum when the field is perpendicular to the coil's face (θ = 0) and zero when the field slides along the face (θ = 90°).

The big idea: a steady flux induces nothing. Only a changing flux produces an induced EMF — move the magnet, change the current, rotate or deform the coil.

Faraday's Law

Faraday's law: whenever the magnetic flux linked with a coil changes, an induced EMF is generated in the coil, and its magnitude is directly proportional to the rate of change of flux. With N turns, each turn adds its own EMF:

EMF = − N × ΔΦ / ΔtN = number of turns, ΔΦ in weber, Δt in seconds → EMF in volts
  • Faster change (smaller Δt) → bigger EMF.
  • Bigger flux change (bigger ΔΦ) → bigger EMF.
  • More turns (bigger N) → bigger EMF.

Lenz's Rule — Which Direction?

Lenz's rule gives the direction of the induced current:

Lenz's rule: the induced current flows in whatever direction makes its own magnetic field oppose the change that produced it.

Push a magnet's N pole toward a coil → the coil's face becomes an N pole to repel you. Pull the magnet away → the face becomes an S pole to attract you and hold you back. The minus sign in Faraday's law is Lenz's rule.

Energy conservation

Lenz's rule is the law of conservation of energy in disguise. The induced current delivers electrical energy, and that energy must come from somewhere: you. You must do mechanical work pushing the magnet against the opposing force, and that work is converted into electrical energy. If the induced current helped the motion instead, we would get free energy forever — which is impossible.

Worked example: A coil of 200 turns experiences a flux change of 5 × 10⁻³ Wb in 0.1 s. Find the induced EMF.
EMF = N ΔΦ/Δt = 200 × (5 × 10⁻³) / 0.1 = 200 × 0.05 = 10 V
