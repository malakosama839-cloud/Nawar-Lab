Chapter 3: Electromagnetic Induction

How changing magnetic fields generate electricity — the physics that powers power stations, electric motors and the grid. Faraday's law is the engine; generators, motors and transformers are its greatest hits.

🧲

Faraday's Law & Lenz's Rule

A changing flux induces an EMF — and the induced current always fights the change.

 〰️

Induction in a Moving Wire

EMF = −B L v sin θ, plus Fleming's right-hand rule for the direction.

 🌀

Mutual & Self Induction

Coupled coils (M) and back-EMF in a coil itself (L), both measured in henry.

 ⚡

The AC Generator (Dynamo)

e = V_m sin(ωt), the peak V_m = NBANω and the RMS value V_m/√2.

 🔄

The DC Motor

Torque τ = BIAN sin θ and the split-ring commutator that keeps it spinning.

 🔝

The Transformer

V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p = I_p/I_s, efficiency, losses, and why we send power at 500 kV.

By the end of this chapter you can…

  • State Faraday's law and Lenz's rule, and explain the minus sign as conservation of energy.
  • Calculate induced EMF from EMF = −N ΔΦ/Δt and EMF = −B L v sin θ.
  • Use Fleming's right-hand rule for induced current (and not confuse it with the left-hand rule).
  • Solve problems with mutual inductance M and self-inductance L (with the definition of the henry).
  • Analyse the AC generator: e = V_m sin(ωt), V_m = NBANω, ω = 2πf and V_eff = V_m/√2.
  • Calculate motor torque τ = BIAN sin θ and explain the split-ring commutator.
  • Solve transformer problems (step-up/step-down, efficiency) and list the energy losses and their remedies.
