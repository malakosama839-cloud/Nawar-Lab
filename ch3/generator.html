The AC Generator (Dynamo)

Structure

An AC generator converts mechanical energy → electrical energy using Faraday's law. Its main parts:

  • A rectangular coil that rotates at constant angular speed ω.
  • A uniform magnetic field between two pole pieces (N and S).
  • Two slip rings fixed to the coil's ends, rotating with it.
  • Two stationary brushes pressing on the slip rings, connecting the coil to the external circuit.

Because the coil rotates, the flux through it changes continuously — a sinusoidal (AC) EMF is induced. Slip rings (unlike the motor's split-ring commutator) keep each end always connected to the same brush, so the output alternates smoothly.

The Equations

As the coil turns, the angle between the coil's normal and B is θ = ωt, so the EMF varies as a sine:

e = Vm × sin θ = Vm × sin (ωt)e = instantaneous EMF (V); θ = ωt is the angle the coil has turned through; ω in rad/s
Vm = N × B × A × ωthe maximum (peak) EMF: N = turns, B in tesla, A = coil area in m², ω = angular speed in rad/s
ω = 2πff = frequency = number of complete rotations per second (Hz)

The effective (RMS) value

An AC voltage with peak Vm delivers the same heating power as a DC voltage of Veff:

Veff (RMS) = Vm / √2 ≈ 0.707 × Vmthis is the value an AC voltmeter reads — e.g. mains "220 V" is the RMS value, with a peak of about 311 V
Worked example 1: A generator gives a peak EMF of 311 V. Find the effective value.
V_eff = V_m / √2 = 311 / 1.414 ≈ 220 V (the familiar mains value!)
Worked example 2: A coil of 100 turns, area 0.02 m², rotates at ω = 100 rad/s in a field of 0.5 T. Find V_m and the frequency.
V_m = N B A ω = 100 × 0.5 × 0.02 × 100 = 100 V
f = ω / 2π = 100 / (2 × 3.1416) ≈ 15.9 Hz
