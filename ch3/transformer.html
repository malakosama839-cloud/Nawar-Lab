The Transformer

Structure & Principle

A transformer changes an AC voltage up or down. It consists of two coils — the primary (connected to the AC source) and the secondary (connected to the load) — wound on the same soft-iron core.

It works by mutual induction: the AC in the primary produces a continuously changing flux in the iron core; that changing flux passes through the secondary and induces an alternating EMF in it. That is why a transformer only works with AC — a steady DC gives a constant flux and induces nothing (except a brief blip when switching on/off).

The Equations

Vs / Vp = Ns / Np = Ip / Isideal (100% efficient) transformer. N_s > N_p → step-UP; N_s < N_p → step-DOWN. Raising the voltage lowers the current, since power is (almost) conserved
η = (Pout / Pin) × 100%efficiency; real transformers reach 95–99%

Step-up vs step-down

  • Step-up: Ns > Np → Vs > Vp, but Is < Ip.
  • Step-down: Ns < Np → Vs < Vp, but Is > Ip.

Energy Losses & How to Reduce Them

LossCauseHow it is reduced
Copper loss (heating)The windings have resistance: P = I²RThick copper wires (low resistance)
Eddy currentsSwirling currents induced in the iron core itself → heatingLaminated core: thin sheets insulated from each other break up the eddy paths
HysteresisEnergy lost re-magnetising the core 100 times per secondSoft iron core (easy to magnetise and demagnetise)
Flux leakageNot all the primary flux passes through the secondaryCore designed so both coils share the same flux path (interleaved windings)

Why high voltage for transmission?

Power lines lose energy as heat: Ploss = I²R. For a given delivered power P = V × I, stepping the voltage up (e.g. to 500 kV) makes the current in the cables small — and since the loss depends on I², it becomes tiny. Near the consumer, step-down transformers return the voltage to safe levels.

Exam favourite: doubling the transmission voltage halves the current and quarters the I²R loss.
Worked example: A transformer has 200 primary turns and 2000 secondary turns. The primary is connected to 220 V and draws 2 A. Find V_s and I_s (assume 100% efficiency).
V_s = V_p × N_s/N_p = 220 × 2000/200 = 2200 V
I_s = I_p × N_p/N_s = 2 × 200/2000 = 0.2 A
Check: P_in = 220 × 2 = 440 W, P_out = 2200 × 0.2 = 440 W ✓ (If η = 95%, P_out = 0.95 × 440 = 418 W.)
