Digital Signals & Binary

Analog vs Digital

Analog signal

The value changes continuously through every possible level — like a dimmer knob or the human voice. At any instant the voltage can be 2.31 V, 2.32 V, 2.313 V…

Digital signal

Only two levels exist: LOW (0) and HIGH (1). There is nothing in between — like a light switch that is either off or on.

Why digital wins

Along a wire, noise and interference slightly distort any signal. A small distortion of an analog signal is the signal — the information is damaged. But in a digital system all that matters is: "is it nearer LOW or nearer HIGH?" A noise wobble of 0.3 V cannot change a 0 into a 1, so the receiver reads the exact original value every time. That is why music, photos, TV and data are all stored digitally.

Tip: digital = two levels (0 / 1), analog = infinitely many levels. Digital is robust because small noise cannot flip a level.

The Binary System

Computers store everything as binary digits (bits). In the binary system each position has a place value that is a power of 2, instead of a power of 10:

… 2⁶ 2⁵ 2⁴ 2³ 2² 2¹ 2⁰
= … 64  32  16  8  4  2  1place values of the bits, from left to right

Binary → decimal: add the place values of the 1-bits

1011₂ = 8 + 0 + 2 + 1 = 11₁₀the little ₂ means "binary", ₁₀ means "decimal"

Decimal → binary: divide by 2, read the remainders backwards

Divide the number by 2 repeatedly, writing each remainder (0 or 1), until you reach 0. The answer is the remainders read from last to first.

Worked example: Convert 25 to binary, then check it back.
25 ÷ 2 = 12 remainder 1
12 ÷ 2 = 6 remainder 0
6 ÷ 2 = 3 remainder 0
3 ÷ 2 = 1 remainder 1
1 ÷ 2 = 0 remainder 1
Reading the remainders from bottom to top: 25 = 11001₂
Check: 11001₂ = 16 + 8 + 0 + 0 + 1 = 16 + 8 + 1 = 25 ✓
